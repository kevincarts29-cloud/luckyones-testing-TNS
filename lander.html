Where the hours actually go

Almost every team we audit loses the same four ways. None of them show up on a dashboard, which is why they tend to persist for years.

Re-keying the same data

Someone exports a report, pastes half of it into another tool, and retypes the rest. It is tedious, it is invisible on any dashboard, and it happens every single week.

Waiting on approvals

A request sits in an inbox for two days because the person who can approve it only checks it on Tuesdays. The delay is rarely anyone's fault and always someone's week.

Rebuilding the same report

The Monday numbers get assembled from scratch every Monday. You cannot trust them quickly, so you stop trusting them, which is its own kind of cost.

Answers that live in someone's head

One person knows how the system works. When they are on leave, everyone else waits. It is the most expensive kind of undocumented process there is.

What the audit gives you

Four deliverables, in plain language. Nothing is held back for a follow-up engagement.

A current-state process map

Every step from trigger to done, including the unofficial ones nobody has written down, with the tools involved at each hand-off.

Hours lost, counted

Per process and per month, with the assumptions written down so you can argue with our numbers. Most audits land between 6 and 40 percent of a headcount.

A ranked fix list

Ordered by hours returned per unit of effort, with a rough build estimate next to each. The top three are usually cheap and quick.

One thing we would not do

The most common finding in a first audit is that the obvious big-ticket tool is the wrong answer. We will say so in writing.

How it runs

Two weeks, four steps, roughly six hours of your team's time in total.

1. Short kickoff call

Ninety minutes with the two or three people who know the work. We go over the process and agree what counts as a good outcome.

2. We map and time it

Over about a week we watch the work happen, read the spreadsheets involved, and measure the actual cycle time rather than the target one.

3. We write it up

Map, hour counts and ranked fixes, written so someone in your team could act on it without us in the room.

4. Walkthrough call

An hour to go through the findings with whoever has to make the call. Then it is yours, whether or not we ever work together again.

Fair questions

The things people ask before they fill in the form.

Is this a sales pitch?

No. It is a paid engagement at a fixed price, and the deliverable is the written plan. Plenty of teams have taken it, read it, and gone elsewhere to build. We would rather that happened than not happen.

How much does it cost?

It is quoted before we start and it does not move. If the audit says a build is not worth it, you have still got a clear answer for a fraction of what a build would have cost.

What do you need from us?

Two or three people who know the process, for the kickoff call and the walkthrough. Plus read access to whatever the work runs through. That is the whole ask.

Will it go near our data?

We look at process and structure. Where a workflow touches customer data we sample shapes and volumes rather than reading live records, and anything we do see is covered by NDA.
