Where this gets used

Same tools, different problems. Here is what teams have actually used them for.

Operations

Order intake, stock movement, and status updates that currently involve a spreadsheet and whoever happens to be on holiday. The win here is usually measured in hours per week, which makes it easy to justify.

  • Order-to-fulfilment automation
  • Exception alerts instead of daily checks
  • Inventory synced across locations
  • Supplier updates without the email chase

Typical result: 30 to 40 hours a week returned to the team.

Finance

Reconciling the same transactions across the bank, the accounting tool, and a spreadsheet someone made in 2019. We automate the matching and leave the judgement calls to your team.

  • Automated transaction matching
  • Month-end reporting that arrives on time
  • Approval chains with a clear audit trail
  • Invoice intake without rekeying

Typical result: close takes days instead of weeks.

Healthcare admin

Administrative paperwork, scheduling, and document handling. We deliberately stay away from clinical decisions. What we build sits behind the scenes so your clinical staff get their time back.

  • Appointment and referral scheduling
  • Document intake and validation
  • Staff rostering and handover notes
  • Audit-ready access logs

Typical result: fewer phone calls, fewer rejected claims.

Retail

Online and offline stock that agree with each other, and reporting that tells you what is actually moving rather than what looks good. The unglamorous middle of commerce, essentially.

  • Stock accuracy across every channel
  • Supplier and purchase order flow
  • Weekly trading summary by location
  • Returns processed without manual entry

Typical result: fewer oversells, faster replenishment.

Manufacturing

Production data that reaches someone before the line stops, rather than after the morning meeting. Sensor feeds are useful, but only if they trigger the right response.

  • Downtime alerts with the cause attached
  • Shift and batch reporting
  • Maintenance scheduling from real usage
  • Quality checks captured on the floor

Typical result: unplanned stops caught earlier.

Internal teams

Onboarding, IT requests, and the endless "how do I do X again" questions. Most companies solve this with a wiki nobody reads, which is a solvable problem.

  • Self-serve onboarding checklists
  • IT request routing and status
  • Docs that answer questions in plain language
  • Access granted automatically on join

Typical result: new hires productive in days, not weeks.
