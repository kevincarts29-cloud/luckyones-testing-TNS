What we do

The six things we spend most of our time on, and roughly how we go about each one.

Product engineering

Web apps and internal tools, built from the first sketch to production support. We keep the feature list short on purpose: everything that ships has to earn its place, and everything that gets cut gets explained rather than quietly dropped.

  • Web apps, dashboards, and internal tools
  • APIs and third-party integrations
  • Design systems so the UI stays consistent
  • Short iterations instead of one big launch
  • Handover, docs, and training for your team

Used on every engagement

Applied AI

We use language models where the time saving is obvious to the person doing the task, and skip them where they would add a review step longer than the work itself. Everything gets tested against your real examples before it goes anywhere near your team.

  • Search and summarisation over your own content
  • Drafting, rewriting, and tone adjustments
  • Ticket and request triage
  • Evaluation against your actual task set
  • Written rules on what the model may do

Used in CopilotKit

Systems & integrations

Most teams do not need another system to manage. They need the ones they have to talk to each other reliably. This is unglamorous work and it is usually where the biggest, fastest wins are hiding.

  • API and webhook integrations
  • Data sync, cleanup, and migration
  • Background jobs and scheduling
  • Error handling, retries, and alerting
  • Coexisting with legacy systems you cannot replace yet

Used in FlowDesk

Data & reporting

Getting the right numbers to the right people on a schedule, formatted so they take seconds rather than an afternoon to read. We also spend a lot of time arguing about metric definitions, which is usually the more valuable half of the job.

  • Agreed, versioned metric definitions
  • Scheduled digests per team
  • Ad-hoc analysis when a question comes up
  • Data quality checks with visible gaps
  • Hand-off into whatever BI tool you already run

Used in ClearView

Security & access

The boring parts, done early: least-privilege access, audit trails, and a dependency patch rhythm that does not depend on anyone remembering. Security that lives in a document nobody reads is not security.

  • SSO and role-based access
  • Dependency and secret scanning in CI
  • Scheduled access reviews
  • Data retention and deletion rules
  • Evidence and docs ready for your audits

Used in VaultID

Support & onboarding

Shipping is the easy part. We help move your data across, sit in on the training session, and answer questions in language your team will actually understand. If something breaks, someone picks it up.

  • Guided onboarding and data migration
  • A named contact, not a ticket queue
  • Runbooks your team can follow alone
  • Training sessions for staff and admins
  • Roadmap reviews as your needs change

Included on every plan

How an engagement usually runs

Nothing here is exotic, which is rather the point. Most projects go better when everyone knows what happens next.

Week 1

Understand

We sit with the people doing the work and map what actually happens today, including the parts nobody put in the process diagram.

Week 2

Scope

You get a written proposal: what we will build, what we are deliberately not building, what it costs, and what we think will go wrong.

Weeks 3-8

Build in the open

Short cycles with a demo at the end of each. Feedback changes the backlog, which is the point.

Week 9

Launch & hand over

Migration, training, and written runbooks. Then we stay on as support if it turns out you want us around.
